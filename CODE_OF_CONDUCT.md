# Code of Conduct

## The short version

Be decent. Assume the other person is acting in good faith. Criticise work,
not people.

## Standards

Things that make this project better:

- Giving feedback on the substance — the code, the finding, the claim — rather
  than the person who wrote it.
- Accepting a correction without treating it as a loss. This is a security
  project; being wrong in public and fixing it is the job.
- Saying what you actually know, and marking what you are unsure of. A
  confident guess about whether something is exploitable wastes everyone's time
  and can get someone hurt.
- Patience with people who are new to the domain. Model supply-chain security
  is young and almost nobody is fluent in all of it.

Things that are not acceptable:

- Harassment, insults, or personal attacks, in issues, pull requests,
  discussions, or private contact arising from this project.
- Sexualised language or imagery, and unwelcome attention of any kind.
- Publishing someone's private information without their explicit permission.
- Sustained disruption — derailing threads, reopening settled decisions without
  new evidence, or demanding unpaid work from maintainers.
- Posting a working exploit against a third party's systems, or using this
  project's issue tracker to coordinate an attack.

## Scope

Applies in every project space — the repository, its discussions, and anywhere
someone is representing the project. It also applies to private contact that
starts here, because "I moved it to email" is not a loophole.

## Reporting

Email **DAVANO@davano.net**. Reports are read by the maintainers and kept
confidential; we will not share your identity with the person reported without
asking you first.

Say what happened, where, and roughly when. Links help. You do not need to
prove anything or arrive with a conclusion — a report that turns out to be a
misunderstanding is not a waste of anyone's time, and we would rather hear it.

We aim to acknowledge within **3 working days**. If you do not hear back in a
week, send it again; that is our failure, not a signal to drop it.

If your report concerns a maintainer, say so, and it will be handled by someone
else.

## Enforcement

Depending on severity and pattern: a private word, a public correction, a
temporary block, or a permanent ban. We will say which and why to the person
concerned. Nobody gets banned for a single clumsy sentence; nobody gets an
indefinite pass for a sustained pattern because their patches are good.
